When COVID's first wave reached India, lockdowns changed everyday life almost overnight. While many of us were staying home, frontline healthcare workers still had to care for patients in difficult and high-risk conditions. Protective equipment was suddenly part of everyone's conversation.

Around the world, and here in India too, the maker community started sharing open designs and finding local ways to make useful things. I love that about makers: someone has an idea, someone else improves it, and people try to help with whatever tools they have.

I had just bought my first 3D printer, an Ender 3, and decided to put it to some DIY hacker use: printing face shields for frontline workers. I was still learning the printer, but this gave it a very real purpose. The printed frame held a clear plastic visor in place, with a bit of cord to help secure it.
